I am **Xiaoyan Li (黎笑妍)**, an MSc student in Geomatics at **The Hong Kong Polytechnic University** and a research assistant in the [3MSI Lab](https://www.3msi.net/), supervised by [Prof. Tiangang Yin](https://www.polyu.edu.hk/lsgs/people/academic-staff/prof-tiangang-yin/).

I am **seeking PhD opportunities for 2027 entry** in forest remote sensing, LiDAR-based 3D reconstruction, and geospatial machine learning.

I study how airborne LiDAR and machine learning can reveal the 3D structure of forests. My current work estimates how woody area is distributed within tree canopies using LiDAR collected while trees are in leaf.

## Research Interests

- **Forest remote sensing and LiDAR.** Reconstructing 3D vegetation structure and estimating woody area from airborne observations.
- **GeoAI and spatiotemporal prediction.** Applying machine learning to geospatial and time-series data for environmental monitoring.
- **Satellite and ecological data integration.** Combining remote sensing observations with ecological records to study forest growth and change.

## Selected Research

### Estimating the 3D Distribution of Forest Woody Area from Leaf-On Airborne LiDAR

*Research Assistant, 3MSI Lab, PolyU · December 2025–present*

I combined NASA G-LiHT airborne LiDAR with terrain and plant area density data across eight forest plots at the Smithsonian Environmental Research Center. I developed a method that distributes each tree's predicted woody area across canopy layers and voxels. On held-out trees, the best configuration improved voxel-level estimates over direct random forest prediction when evaluated against estimates derived from leaf-off LiDAR.

**Manuscript** — Li, X., Zhou, C., Wang, J., & Yin, T. (2026). *Estimating the 3D Distribution of Forest Woody Area from Leaf-On Airborne LiDAR.* Submission in progress.

### Qinghai Spruce Radial Growth and Satellite NDVI in the Helan Mountains

*BSc thesis, Northwest Normal University · 2025*

I combined tree-ring, satellite, and climate records to compare Qinghai spruce growth at two elevations. Radial growth had weak associations with satellite NDVI at both sites and stronger associations with early-season precipitation and later-season temperature.

### Ecological Protection and Economic Development in the Yellow River Basin

*Research Assistant, Northwest Normal University · March–June 2024*

I helped estimate ecosystem service values with InVEST, analyze regional patterns of ecological and economic indicators, and build an ArcGIS Engine platform to map the results.
